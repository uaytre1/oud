# How oud is taught, and what it means for the app (research, 2026-11-27)

The owner: "research more about oud from oud teachers ... this app should be like a teacher, better than one, who is
always available. How do they teach oud, the progress and process, and search more about the instrument." Sources are
listed at the end; most are course pages, method books and teachers' sites (no single standard curriculum exists).

## The instrument (facts to keep the app right)
- Usually 11 strings in 6 courses (5 doubled courses + 1 single bass); some ouds have 5 or 7 courses.
- Scale length: **Arabic oud 61-62 cm**, Turkish about 58.5 cm. (The neck drawing in the app is to scale by proportion,
  so the exact length does not change where the dots sit relative to each other.)
- Fretless: quarter tones (the half-flats of Bayati, Rast, Saba), slides and vibrato come from that. Many beginners put
  tape markers on the fingerboard and take them off as the ear and the hand learn. The app's neck dots are virtual tape
  markers: same idea, nothing on the real oud.
- Tuning: in fourths, with the lowest courses varying by region and player (the app's Settings presets already cover
  Standard Arabic, Bayati bass, Egyptian, Traditional, Syrian).
- The risha (plectrum, "feather"): held against the fingers, pressed by the thumb; today usually plastic.
- Tremolo (fast down-up on one note) is one of the most typical sounds of the Arabic oud style.

## How teachers order it (a typical progression, not one official syllabus)
1. The instrument, how to hold the oud and the risha.
2. Right hand only on open strings (long and short notes), then both hands on open strings.
3. Risha strokes: down ("Ta"), then down-up ("Ta-Ti"), alternating across open strings with a metronome, before the
   left hand gets complicated. Triplets later as down-up-down.
4. Left hand: one string at a time, finding the notes; then simple exercises.
5. Maqamat one by one, each followed at once by simple songs in that maqam. One course order: Kurd, Nahawand, Bayati,
   Saba, Hijaz, Rast; other teachers start with Hijaz (easy to hear) or Rast (quarter tones in a measured way).
6. Ornaments and tremolo once the basics are solid; taqsim (improvising in the maqam) much later.
Method books: Charbel Rouhana's method (Arabic and English, 5 levels; his conservatory aims at 8), Marina Toshich
"Basics of Oud", Hal Leonard "Oud Method", Michel Merhej Baklouk "School of Oud" (makam theory), the Mukhtar Method
(the eight fundamental maqamat and popular repertoire).

## How teachers say to practise
- Short daily sessions beat long rare ones; consistency matters most; quality over length (bad practice builds bad habits).
- A common order inside a session: maqam/scales → ear → technique → repertoire.
- Learn maqamat by ear: listen to many melodies in the maqam, sing a short phrase, then find it on the oud. It takes
  repeated listening over a long time.
- Loop the hard bit slowly, then speed it up (also how Rocksmith's Riff Repeater works; see quality.md research).

## What the app already does from this
| Teachers do | The app |

| Hold, then right hand on open strings, then strokes | Path stage 1 (hold, tune, strike), risha trainer (1 per beat, down-up) |
| One maqam then songs in it | The 8 maqamat (Slowly, In time), songs with their maqam |
| Tape markers until the ear knows | The neck: dashed ring where the finger goes, dim dots for the line's other notes |

| Short daily practice | Daily practice (about 10 minutes, 4 items), this week's days on Home |

## What to add next (from this research; owner asked 2026-11-27 for the first three)
1. **Maqam training on loop**: a scale repeats by itself; In time speeds up after each clean round (60% → 100%).
2. **Practise these notes**: from Check my take, loop just the notes that went wrong, slowly.
3. **Maqam card** on each song: its notes on the neck, the tonic (home note) marked, "simple songs in this maqam" next.
4. Later, ear training the teachers' way: hear a short phrase of the song, hum it, then find it (no score; just listen,
   hum, play).
5. Later, tremolo drills with the listener counting even picks.
On hold (owner): a tolerance that tightens as he improves.
